# Rule ledger: example pack

**Fictional example.** A real ledger records what the author has actually cut from a draft, cites the piece where it was observed, and marks how strong the evidence is. Entries are retired the same way they are added.

`established` means it has held more than once or survived a test. `weak` means one sighting.

## Don't add these

| Rule | Strength | Where it was observed |
|---|---|---|
| No "it's worth noting" or similar run-ups. Cut twice in one draft. | established | viaduct-joints piece, r2 and r4 |
| Don't convert a measurement into a metaphor ("the deck was tired"). | established | viaduct-joints piece, r3 |
| Don't close a section on a summary line; the author ends on the recommendation. | weak | culvert-survey note, r2 |

## Flag these even when they have survived

| Rule | Strength | Where it was observed |
|---|---|---|
| A figure that paraphrases the author's own earlier piece must match it. | established | culvert-survey note, where a load figure drifted between two posts |
| A line that came from a brief rather than the author stays a question until the author explicitly adopts it; surviving revisions isn't adoption. | established | pack convention |

## Their register, not slop

| Pattern | Strength | Where it was observed |
|---|---|---|
| Semicolons joining a measurement to its implication. | established | measured across 12 pieces |
| "Nobody was careless" style refusals of easy blame. | established | viaduct-joints piece |
