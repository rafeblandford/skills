# Shareable example: Repair Relay

Use this neutral example only when a concrete illustration helps select a format or understand how one idea advances through the system. Do not copy its composition into unrelated concepts.

## Common brief

```yaml
idea_name: Repair Relay
idea: A household repair product that turns a photograph of a broken object into an inspectable diagnosis and a guided repair path.
audience: People who want to repair an item but do not know whether the job is safe, economical or within their ability.
decision_to_support: Decide whether to attempt the repair, find help or replace the object.
current_situation: Repair advice is fragmented across videos, manuals, parts shops and local services.
current_friction: The user cannot easily judge the diagnosis, risk, required part or next action.
what_if: What if a photograph became a repair path the user could inspect and challenge?
value_promised: Greater confidence about what is broken and what to do next without hiding uncertainty.
decisive_interaction: The user confirms or rejects each diagnostic clue before the product recommends a repair route.
system_response: The product presents evidence, confidence, safety boundaries, parts and local help as separate atoms.
changed_outcome: The user chooses a repair route with visible reasons and can stop before ordering or attempting anything.
important_uncertainty: Whether users understand the difference between a plausible diagnosis and a verified fault.
question_to_provoke: Does visible diagnostic uncertainty build confidence or make the product feel less useful?

truth:
  known:
    - A photograph alone may not establish a fault safely.
  proposed:
    - Each diagnostic clue has evidence and confidence.
    - The user confirms before parts are ordered or instructions begin.
  assumed:
    - Relevant manuals and parts information can be retrieved.
  to_learn:
    - Whether people inspect the reasoning before choosing a repair route.

brand_or_context: Neutral consumer service
must_show:
  - A visible safety boundary.
  - The user challenging one clue.
  - Repair, local help and replacement as distinct outcomes.
must_avoid:
  - Implying the photograph provides a definitive diagnosis.
  - Automatically ordering parts.
evidence_or_sources: []
format: auto
house_style: editorial-instrument@2
output: single
```

## How it changes by format

### Proposition sketch

One dominant phone frame shows the photographed object and three diagnostic clues. A hand-drawn arrow opens three outcome fragments: repair it, take it to local help, or replace it. The safety boundary is highlighted.

### Mechanism board

The diagnosis, evidence atoms, safety gate and three routes are separate vector groups so a workshop can rearrange the model and challenge where confirmation belongs.

### Product storyboard

A believable product screen shows one clue selected, why the system thinks it matters, and the user action `Confirm clue` or `Not this`. The proposition is supported by two annotations and the uncertainty is explicit.

### Interactive prototype

Three states demonstrate the behaviour:

1. photograph and describe the problem;
2. inspect and challenge the proposed diagnosis;
3. choose a repair route with evidence and safety boundaries attached.

The prototype uses local illustrative data and performs no real diagnosis or purchase.
