# Profile: example pack

**This is a fictional example pack.** Every name, figure and passage in it is invented, to show the shape a real pack takes. It is nobody's voice: the skills treat it as no pack unless you ask them to demonstrate on it. Copy the structure; replace all of it. `README.md` says how.

A real pack is built from the author's own writing, measured rather than guessed, and private.

## Who is writing

A fictional civil engineer who writes about infrastructure maintenance for a professional audience of planners and contractors. Writes in British English, from fifteen years of site experience, and would rather be exact than quotable.

## The eight constants, each with its edge

The left column is the habit worth keeping. The right column is the failure that looks like it, which is the more useful half: a drafter imitating the habit usually produces the right-hand version.

| This is them | This isn't them |
|---|---|
| **Evidence before position.** Gives the measurement, then what they conclude from it. | Leading with the conclusion and attaching a number afterwards. |
| **Names the trade-off.** Says what was given up for what was gained. | Presenting a decision as costless, or listing pros and cons without choosing. |
| **Specific to the site.** Every general claim is anchored to a job they worked on. | A general claim with an invented or borrowed example. |
| **Credits by name.** Says who did the work. | Passive constructions that hide who decided. |
| **Exact about uncertainty.** Says what isn't known yet and what would settle it. | Qualifying every sentence until nothing is claimed, or stating an inference as a measurement. |
| **Reasons in the sentence.** The "because" and the "so" sit inside one sentence with the claim they support. | The same reasoning chopped into short declaratives that leave the reader to join them. |
| **Reports what went wrong plainly.** A defect is described as found, including their own. | A confession performed for effect, or a lesson nobody actually drew. |
| **Writes for the person who acts on it.** A recommendation says who does what, and by when. | Jargon used to sound expert, or advice too general to act on. |

Admissions and lessons are present or absent, never required: a piece without one is not missing anything.

## How they label AI involvement

Fictional convention for this example: posts carry a footer saying whether a draft was AI-assisted. Only unassisted pieces count as voice evidence; assisted ones are useful as contrast.

## Tiers

Tier 1 is site notes and email. Tier 2 is short public writing. Tier 3 is anything with their name on it that a client will read closely. The tier sets how hard provenance is checked, not how good the writing has to be.
