# Register: essay (example pack)

**Fictional example.** The passages below are invented. A real register file quotes the author verbatim, with source, date, provenance and word count, and never borrows from anyone else.

Long-form writing under their own name. Tier 3 by default. Read with `profile.md` and `craft.md`. The two exemplars are unassisted and 100–250 words each (138 and 164), so the tier-3 comparative read can use them as references.

## Exemplar 1: "What the 1974 joints tell us"

Fictional publication, 4 March 2026 · unassisted · verbatim, 138 words

> The expansion joints on that viaduct were specified in 1974 for a traffic load we now exceed every weekday morning. Nobody was careless; the forecast was simply wrong, and the detail that carried it has been working harder than intended for fifty years. When we lifted the deck in February we found the elastomer had taken a permanent set, which is what you would expect, and the holding-down bolts were sound, which you would not. Priya Shah's team had replaced them in 1998 using a specification nobody asked for, and that decision is the reason the structure needs a new bearing rather than a new span. The lesson I take from it is that the failure you plan for is rarely the one that arrives, and the margin someone adds quietly is often the one that saves you.

What it does: gives the measurement before the judgement, refuses the easy blame ("nobody was careless"), reports the surprising finding as surprising, credits the people by name, and closes on a position rather than a flourish.

## Exemplar 2: "Inspect the culverts in August"

Fictional publication, 19 May 2026 · unassisted · verbatim, 164 words

> We inspect culverts in February because that is when the contract says to, and February is the month when you can see least. The water is high, the inverts are under it, and the scour that matters is the scour you can't reach. Last year we moved six of our forty inspections to August as a trial. In four of the six we found undermining that the previous three winter inspections had recorded as "no change"; in two we found nothing new. That is a small sample, and I would not rewrite a framework on it. But the cost of the move was a revised access plan and one week of a second crew, against the cost of a collapsed headwall, which on the A-road job last spring was eleven weeks of single-lane working. I would move the inspections of any culvert with a history of scour to late summer, starting next year, and keep the rest where they are until we know more.

What it does: states the practice, then why it fails; reports the trial with its denominator and its null results; says plainly how far the evidence goes; names the trade-off in cost; and ends on a recommendation someone can act on, with its scope.

## Register notes

1. **Open on the finding**, at around 18 words, not on the writer.
2. **Hedge the inference, never the measurement.** A number is given plainly; what it means is qualified.
3. **No question closes.** None in 12 published pieces.
4. **Every general claim names a job.** A claim with no site attached is not theirs.
5. **Credit by name.** Passive constructions that hide the decision-maker get flagged.
6. **Say how far the evidence goes.** Small samples are called small, once, in a sentence of their own.
