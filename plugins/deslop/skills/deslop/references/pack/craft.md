# Craft: example pack

**Fictional example.** The figures below are invented to show the form; a real `craft.md` reports rates measured from the author's own corpus, with the sample size beside them and the tool that measured them.

Rates are per 1,000 words, measured over 12 published pieces (14,000 words), 9 short posts (2,300 words) and 40 sent emails (9,000 words).

## Openings

| | Long pieces | Email |
|---|---|---|
| Opening sentence, median words | 18 | 9 |
| Opens with a measurement | 7 of 12 | 2 of 40 |

A long piece opens on the finding. Email opens by placing the reader.

## Evidence

- Quotes a specification or a standard by number rather than paraphrasing it: 3.1 per 1,000 words.
- Attributes with a verb ("the report states", "the survey found") rather than a bracketed citation, and always names the source.
- Hedges the inference, never the measurement: "the deck is sound; whether it lasts another decade depends on the joint detail".

## Construction: how the sentences are built

Surface rates (punctuation, length, vocabulary) say little about whether a draft reasons like the author. These say more. A real pack measures them with a parser and word lists, and states what each list counts.

| Per 1,000 words, unless marked | Long pieces | Short posts | Email |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clauses per sentence (mean) | 2.4 | 2.0 | 1.8 |
| Sentences that are a single clause | 31% | 38% | 44% |
| Contrast ("but", "however", "although") | 9.1 | 6.8 | 5.5 |
| Trade-offs ("at the cost of", "in exchange for") | 2.7 | 1.4 | 0.6 |
| Hedges ("probably", "I think", "may") | 5.2 | 4.0 | 7.9 |
| Boosters ("clearly", "certainly") | 1.1 | 1.6 | 0.9 |
| Reader address ("you") | 3.0 | 8.4 | 21.0 |

What a drafter should take from it: the reasoning is carried inside sentences rather than split across them, a contrast or a trade-off appears in most paragraphs, and commitment is high on measurements and lower on predictions. These are descriptions, never quotas.

## Between their speech and the page

The author dictates site notes on the drive back and writes long pieces at a desk. When a draft is assembled from dictation, about one word in ten survives into the published text; the argument survives far more often than the wording. Written notes survive almost word for word.

So, when revising: words they wrote are carried over as they are; dictated words may be tidied (filler removed, grammar repaired), but not swapped for generic phrasing.

## Stability

A real pack benefits from measuring an older body of the author's writing against the current one, to separate long-standing habits from this year's preferences. In this fictional author's case the semicolon habit and the measurement-first opening appear in both; the reader address in short posts is recent. Lean on the habits that appear in both.
