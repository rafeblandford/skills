# What they change when they edit: example pack

**Fictional example.** A real `edits.md` is built from the author's own draft-and-published pairs, which is the strongest evidence a pack can hold: what someone keeps is partly accident, what they reliably remove is a decision.

## How to build this file

1. Collect pairs: the draft as it was handed over, and the text as published or sent.
2. Diff each pair and record what changed, by kind rather than by instance.
3. **Hold some pairs back.** Learn the rules from most of them, then check whether those rules predict the ones you never looked at. A rule that only describes the pairs it came from is a description, not a rule.
4. Mark any rule with no test case as untested, and say so in the register notes that depend on it.

## Example results

Eight pairs to learn from, three held back.

| Behaviour | Learned from 8 | Held-out 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Removes an opening rhetorical question | 3 of 3 | 1 of 1 |
| Replaces a metaphor with the measurement | 4 of 5 | 2 of 2 |
| Keeps semicolons the drafter removed | 6 of 8 | 3 of 3 |
| Shortens the piece | 5 of 8 | 1 of 3 |

Across all eleven pairs, 61% of published sentences survive verbatim from the draft, and the median length change is 40 words shorter.
