# Tier 1: residue

The whole of a tier-1 pass. Read this file and stop; the structural and provenance work belongs to tiers 2 and 3. Nothing here needs the author's voice pack.

**Whose words first.** Tier 1 does no provenance audit, but it does respect ownership: if the caller says the author wrote the text, or it is plainly their own message, nothing below is a cut. At most it is a *their call* note, and an admission or hedge they wrote is kept. Otherwise, propose cutting, in this order:

1. **Chatbot residue.** "Great question", "Certainly", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like me to", "As an AI", any offer of further assistance, any restatement of the request back to the reader.
2. **Throat-clearing openers.** "Here's the thing", "Here's what I find interesting", "The truth is", "It turns out", "Let me be clear", "I'm going to be honest with you". State the point instead.
3. **Disclaimers a drafter added.** Boilerplate hedges about standing, apologies for length, warnings that a view is personal when the byline already says so. The author's own admissions ("I'm no expert, but", "I don't understand this area well") are theirs: keep them. A genuine scope caveat about evidence is not a disclaimer either.
4. **Decorative formatting.** Bold on a phrase that carries no more weight than the sentence around it, a heading over two sentences, an emoji used as ornament rather than as a signpost, a bulleted list of three short noun phrases that would read better as a sentence.
5. **Emphasis crutches.** "Full stop.", "Let that sink in.", "Make no mistake", "This matters because" where the next clause already shows why.
6. **Mannered prose.** See the section below. Say what you mean; keep a metaphor only when the author wrote it.
7. **Sign-off furniture.** A closing line that summarises what the reader has just read, a call to action nobody requested, a question added only to invite replies.

Leave alone:

- Anything inside a quotation, a title, a proper name, or a passage discussing the phrase itself.
- The author's own sign-offs, greetings and habitual openers. In correspondence these are register, not residue.
- A hedge that scopes a claim. Removing it changes the meaning, which is an error, not a tidy.

Stop when the list above is clear. If you find yourself reaching for structural tells, the piece wants tier 2, and that is the author's decision rather than yours.

## Mannered prose

The paragraph is Anthropic's, quoted from the "Writing density" section of its [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) guidance. It is quoted for reference and is not covered by this skill's licence.

> Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement. Instead of "a parameter worth varying," the mannered writer produces "a dial worth turning." Instead of "this point still matters," they write "this point earns its keep." The phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell. That is why mannered prose irritates: it makes the reader work harder so the writer can perform. It is also imprecise. Metaphors drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control. The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it.

**The author's own images are the exception.** The rule applies to figures of speech a drafter would be adding, not to one the author wrote or said. An image that arrives with an attribution ("in their phrase, like fitting a jet engine to a bicycle") also stays, with the attribution attached. Where the caller, the context or (at tier 2 and up) the voice pack says an image is the author's own, treat it as theirs.
