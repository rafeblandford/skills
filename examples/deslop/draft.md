# Short post: the Mill Lane footbridge reopening

Invented fixture for the deslop decision page. The author, bridge and parish are fictional.

## Notes

- Spring inspection flagged four of the twenty-two joists. Only those four were cored.
- Programme: nine-week closure; reopened after three.

## Post (r1)

We reopened the Mill Lane footbridge this morning, three weeks into a nine-week programme, which is a nice thing to be able to write.

The background: the bridge's timber deck had been flagged in the spring inspection for rot in four of the twenty-two joists. The original plan was to replace the whole deck, which meant closing the crossing for nine weeks and sending pedestrians on a **1.4 km** detour.

The survey told a different story. Coring each joist showed the rot was confined to the bearing ends, where water had been sitting on the abutment shelf. Replacing the bearing ends simply solved the problem without touching the rest of the deck... and the new `weep-hole` drainage stops it coming back.

Credit goes to the site team, who did the coring in a single weekend this month. Thank you, all.

Why does six weeks matter? Because a nine-week closure on a school route is the kind of thing that turns a maintenance job into a council agenda item, and nobody's deck is improved by that.

The full report goes to the parish council at next month's meeting.
